There are a few. They're not strict, but they exist.

## The actual rules

**The plate has to be legible.** If we can't read it, we can't validate it, and it doesn't enter the catalog. A blurry plate isn't a sighting — it's an aspiration.

**It has to be a Fisker Ocean.** We validate every plate against the NYC TLC database and against Fisker VINs (which start with `VCF1`). If the plate belongs to a Polestar, we'll catch it.

**Don't fake locations.** GPS comes from your photo's EXIF data. Don't strip it, don't fabricate it.

**Don't be a jerk to drivers.** These are working vehicles. Most Oceans on NYC streets are someone's livelihood. Don't slow them down, don't get in their way, don't be weird.

**Don't put yourself in traffic.** A photo isn't worth a hospital bill.

## Things that are not rules

- You don't have to live in NYC.
- You don't have to be first to count.
- You don't have to use any particular app or service.
- You don't have to take a "good" photo — a passable one is fine.

## What about duplicates?

Duplicates are fine. The catalog distinguishes between first sightings (worth [Ocean Points](/p/ocean-points), ◎p) and subsequent ones. Seeing the same Ocean twice earns you the [Self-Dupe](/badges) badge. Photographing one from inside another earns you a story worth telling.
